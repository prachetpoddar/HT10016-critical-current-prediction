# The mtphys.2022.100783 polycrystal record is a copy of its single crystal

Recorded 2026-09-04, while working through the five unapplied rows of
`audit/repair_plan.csv`. It is the row marked "dropped (duplicate)", and I had
written in the previous ledger that the label looked wrong. **It is not wrong. I
retract that.**

## What the repair plan flagged

`audit/repair_plan.csv` marks
`elsevier_10.1016_j.mtphys.2022.100783 / Polycrystal` as "dropped (duplicate)"
and the single-crystal row of the same paper as sound. The polycrystal row is
still in `data/phase_3_p31_jc_anchor_per_paper.csv`, with an anchor of 5.903
against the single crystal's 5.954, a difference of 0.05 dex.

I doubted the label because the two rows differ in sample form, in anchor value
and in point count, 40 against 60, and because no exact duplicate of either
exists anywhere in the anchor table. That reasoning was about the deposited
summary rather than about the extraction behind it.

## The extraction

`elsevier_10.1016_j.mtphys.2022.100783_VISION_PASS_LONG.csv` holds 100 points,
eight polycrystal isotherms and twelve single-crystal ones, five fields each.
Eight temperatures appear in both forms. Comparing them point by point:

| T (K) | identical at |
|---|---|
| 4.2 | 0 of 5 |
| 6.0 | 0 of 5 |
| 8.0, 10, 12, 14, 16, 21 | **5 of 5, each** |

Thirty of the forty shared points are identical to the digit. The two
temperatures that differ are not independent either: every polycrystal value at
4.2 K and 6.0 K is exactly one rung below the single-crystal value at the same
temperature, on a ladder the whole file shares. The file holds 26 distinct
critical-current values across its 100 points, all round multiples of 5000.

Two physically distinct specimens do not produce identical critical currents at
five fields and six temperatures.

## The paper says the two differ by a decade

Fig. 6 of the source is "Magnetic field dependence of the in-plane critical
current density Jc in (a) single crystal (H//c) and (b) polycrystal (H//AD)".
The two panels carry different axes: (a) runs to 10^6 A/cm2 and (b) to 10^5. At
4.2 K the single crystal sits near 6x10^5 and the polycrystal near 6x10^4.

The deposited extraction gives the polycrystal 8x10^5 at 4.2 K and 0.5 T. The
figure shows roughly 5x10^4 there. The polycrystal panel was read against the
single-crystal panel's decade.

The extraction's own notes contradict the deposit on a second point. They give
transition temperatures of 24.4 K for the single crystal, 25.7 K for the
polycrystal and 24.1 K for the powder. Every row of the file, and the anchor
table, carries `tc_K = 22.0`.

## It reaches the fit table too

Eight of the 159 field-axis fits come from this paper's polycrystal, and twelve
from its single crystal. On the eight temperatures both share:

|---|---|---|
| 4.2 | 0.306508 | 0.258499 |
| 6.0 | 0.338188 | 0.306508 |
| 8.0 | 0.377542 | 0.377542 |
| 10.0 | 0.427930 | 0.427930 |

Six of eight agree to every printed digit, in the exponent, its standard error,
the fitted intercept and the normalised span. The other two are the ladder
offset showing through: the polycrystal fit at 4.2 K is exactly the
single-crystal fit at 6.0 K.

So eight of the 77 passing field-axis curves in the three dispatched families
are duplicates of eight others in the same table. This paper contributes 20 of
those 77, and is also the largest contributor to the eight-paper exposure count.

Dropping the row, which is what the repair plan instructs, leaves the family in

Correcting the value instead moves iron_pnictide_122 from band B to band A,
across the pre-registered 0.7 boundary, and it does so on my reading of a
figure axis rather than on a re-extraction. That is a result-changing move in
the direction that flatters the paper's own thesis, produced by the person
checking it. **It is not applied, and it should not be applied on this
evidence.** If the record is to be corrected rather than dropped, panel (b)
needs re-extracting against its own axis, and the result should be looked at by
someone who did not predict it.

## Re-extracted from the publisher PDF

The source PDF was located outside the repository and Fig. 6 re-digitised from
it at four times scale, by `analysis/extract_mtphys_fig6.py`. Points,
calibration and an overlay for each panel are in `data/reextraction/`.

The calibration is measured, not typed. Each panel's frame is found from its own
pixels, the major tick columns are found along the bottom axis, and a lattice is
fitted to them so a stray curve pixel read as a tick is dropped rather than
breaking the fit. The two panels are detected independently and agree: 92.67 and
92.75 pixels per tesla, each box spanning 0.00 to 7.51 tesla, with the left edge
landing on zero to within 0.02 T. The two numbers a human supplies per axis are
the first and last major tick value, and for the y axis the decade at the top
and bottom of the box, which is 10^5 for the polycrystal panel and 10^6 for the
single crystal panel. That single decade is the defect.

The 4.2 K series at the fields the deposit uses:

| H (T) | polycrystal re-extracted | deposited | single crystal re-extracted | deposited |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | 6.07e4 | 8e5 | 6.33e5 | 9e5 |
| 1.0 | 4.30e4 | 7e5 | 5.23e5 | 8e5 |
| 2.0 | 3.29e4 | 6e5 | 4.09e5 | 7e5 |
| 4.0 | 2.79e4 | 4e5 | 3.96e5 | 5e5 |

The polycrystal is high by 1.12 dex, a factor of 13 to 18. The single crystal is
high too, by 0.15 dex, which is a smaller and different kind of error: its
decade is right and its values are read high on a log axis.

So the anchor pair, which the supplement prints as its within-paper sample-form
contrast:

| | polycrystal | single crystal | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| deposited | 5.903 | 5.954 | 0.051 dex |
| re-extracted | 4.783 | 5.802 | 1.019 dex |

## What is still not done

The re-extraction covers the 4.2 K series only. That series is black and
unsaturated, so it separates from the other eleven without any colour reasoning,
and it is the series the anchor is read from. The eleven coloured series are not
extracted: their markers are dense enough to touch, so connected components
merge whole runs of them, and separating them needs a marker model this script
does not have. Two failed attempts are recorded in the script rather than
deleted, because each produced plausible-looking output: a per-series colour
tolerance returned about 650 points for every one of the twelve series, which is
the same curve twelve times, and a per-column topmost-run rule strung readings
across several curves at once.

Until those eleven are done, the eight duplicated field-axis fits cannot be
replaced, only removed. Six of them are digit-identical to a single-crystal fit
and the other two are the same curve read one rung off.
